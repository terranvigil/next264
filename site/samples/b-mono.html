01The bit budget

One second of uncompressed 1080p, 4:2:0, sixty frames a second, is 187 megabytes. That is 1.5 gigabits per second, for one stream, before anyone has done anything clever.

A good 1080p60 stream is about 6 Mbit/s. So the job is to discard 249 out of every 250 bits and have nobody notice. Everything below is a method for choosing which 249.

02Four redundancies

Compression is bookkeeping against four kinds of waste. Every standardised codec attacks the same four, in the same order.

  • Spatial. Neighbouring pixels look alike. Predict a block from the pixels already decoded above and to the left; code only the difference.
  • Temporal. Frame 41 is mostly frame 40, moved. Find where each block went and send a motion vector instead of a picture.
  • Statistical. After prediction, what is left is mostly zero. An entropy coder spends fractions of a bit on common values, more on rare ones.
  • Perceptual. Eyes weigh brightness over colour, and gradients over texture detail. Spend the bits where they get seen.
the one that is a judgement call The first three are free: done properly they cost no quality at all. The fourth is an opinion about human vision. Two encoders at the same bitrate are mostly disagreeing about perception.

03The encode loop

Every block goes around the same circuit. Predict, subtract, transform, quantise, entropy code — then the encoder decodes its own output and keeps that, because the reconstruction is all the decoder will ever have to predict from.

04Where the quality goes

Prediction, transform and entropy coding are all reversible. Exactly one step destroys information: quantisation — divide each transform coefficient by a step size, round, move on. QP selects that step, and the step doubles every six QP.

Frames leave the encoder out of display order so a B-frame can see the future. That reordering is exactly what live encoding cannot always afford.

06Rate control

Quantisation gives one knob per block. Rate control is the policy that sets it thousands of times a second, blind to the future, against a target it is not allowed to miss.

  • CQP — fix the knob, let the bitrate land where it lands. For experiments.
  • CRF — hold quality steady, let the bitrate move. The sane default.
  • ABR — hit an average over the file. Needs lookahead to know what is coming.
  • VBV — never overflow the decoder's buffer. What broadcast and ABR streaming demand.
why lookahead pays An encoder that has seen the next fifty frames knows the quiet shot is about to cut to an explosion, and starts saving. One that has not will spend the budget on the calm and starve the thing you were actually watching.

07Measuring it

PSNR measures squared error and disagrees mildly with human eyes. SSIM looks at structure. VMAF is a learned model fitted to human ratings — closest to useful, with its own blind spots.

Comparing two encoders takes more than one number, because rate and quality trade against each other. BD-rate integrates the gap between two rate/quality curves: −5% means the same quality for 5% fewer bits over the measured range. It is half a comparison. Always read it next to the wall-clock time it cost.
