# The three goals on the ten-clip board, 2026-09-02

One machine, one day, the ten clips of `scripts/parity-clips.sh` (three CIF, four 720p, three 1080p), `scripts/ffboard.py` after its 2026-09-02 fixes: the clip list is read from `parity-clips.sh`, timed encodes write to `/dev/null`, every tier solves its CRF at the thread count it is measured at (goal 1 at one thread), preset medium, 6-second window, median of 3 samples. Encoder at main `65b576b` (the `parity-push` merge); x264 r3223 from `/tmp/x264asm` (SIMD) and `/tmp/x264noasm` (pure C, `-fno-tree-vectorize` stripped). Raw log: `local/records/board-2026-09-02.log`. CRF only: it is the mode the goals are read in, and the review that produced this board (`docs/parity-review-2026-09-01.md`) is where the ABR half lives.

This is the first published board with 1080p clips in it. Every goal figure before this date came from the six legacy clips (`CLIPS=legacy` reproduces that set), which is why the numbers here are higher than the 0.95x / 0.85x / 0.96x that `site/results.md` carried until now: they are a harder board, not a regression.

## How to read these numbers

Say which board a figure came from: this one, or the six-clip legacy set every earlier board used. The two are not comparable, and the legacy six has no 1080p clip. Do not compare a number here to one from another day beyond its spread; the median moves about 0.05 and single cells about 0.03 between rounds on the same build (`docs/parity-review-2026-09-01.md` section 10 has three rounds of the pre-merge board). `CPU work` is our CPU seconds over x264's for the same cell: at auto threads we reach the wall ratio with 1.15 to 1.30x the CPU, and the auto budget hands x264 more threads than us (its 1080p cells run 12.6 to 15.0 cores against our 14.1 to 16.0).
