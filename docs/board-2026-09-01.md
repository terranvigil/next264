# The three goals in CRF, ABR and rate-matched ABR

One machine, one day, six clips, `scripts/ffboard.py`, preset medium, 6-second
window, median of 3 samples. This file exists because the ABR half of the goal
table was never a speed reading and now is one.

## The tables

| goal | configuration | CRF | ABR | ABR rate-matched |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | pure C, single-threaded | 1.05x | 1.20x | **1.09x** |
| 2 | pure C, auto-threaded | 0.93x | 1.28x | **1.26x** |
| 3 | as-shipped SIMD, auto-threaded | 0.99x | 1.41x | **1.40x** |

dsize, which is what makes the middle column unreadable and the right one
readable:

| goal | ABR dsize | rate-matched dsize |
|---|--:|--:|
| 1 | +2.7% | +0.2% |
| 2 | +3.9% | +0.0% |
| 3 | +3.9% | +0.0% |

## What RC=abrm does

`RC=abr` hands both encoders the same TARGET. They land on different achieved
rates, and emitting more bits costs time, so the ratio partly reports which
encoder spent less. `RC=abrm` solves x264's target so it lands on whatever
yah264 achieved, the same trick `RC=crf` already used on the rate factor. dsize
collapsing to zero is the proof it worked.

At matched bits the dVMAF also gets worse -- goal 1 from -0.26 to -0.93, goals 2
and 3 from +0.32 to -0.59 -- because the old reading was buying its quality with
2.7 to 3.9% more bits.

## The result, and it is not the one that was expected

**Removing the bit term moves the ratio by 0.11, 0.02 and 0.01.** The bit
difference was never the main story. ABR still reads 1.09x, 1.26x and 1.40x
where CRF on the same day reads 1.05x, 0.93x and 0.99x.

Subtract them and the shape is unmistakable:

| goal | rate-matched ABR minus CRF |
|---|--:|
| 1, single-threaded | +0.04 |
| 2, auto-threaded | **+0.33** |
| 3, auto-threaded | **+0.41** |

**At one thread ABR costs us essentially nothing over CRF. At auto threads it
costs a third to four tenths of the ratio.** Two encoders doing the same work on
the same number of bits, and the only difference is that one of them is running
a rate-control feedback loop -- so what that loop costs is not arithmetic, it is
SERIALIZATION. It shows up only when there are threads for it to hold up.

That is a specific, sized target and it was invisible while the bit term sat on
top of it. It also lines up with what the threading track already believes:
the mb-tree barrier's serial share grows with thread count, and the goal-2 tail
was sleep-billing rather than pool-idle.

## How to read these numbers

Say which of the three columns a figure came from. They are three different
questions and the CRF one is the mode most people encode in.

Do not compare a number here to one from another day. The day-to-day spread on
a median reaches about 0.10, which is wider than several of the margins in the
table -- goal 3's CRF reading was 0.96x on 2026-08-27, 1.01x on 2026-08-31 and
0.99x here, on builds that measure identically.

`RC=abr` remains the default so every historical figure this board produced
still reproduces.
